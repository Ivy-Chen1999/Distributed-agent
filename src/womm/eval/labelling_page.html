    Help us check an AI-written answer key

    Our AI lists who a new EU law affects and how. We grade it against an answer key that another AI wrote from the EU's official reports. An AI cannot be trusted to check another AI, so a person must check the key before we use it. That is you.

    Why a person has to look

    • Every grade depends on the key. A wrong key rewards our AI for copying its mistakes, and we could not tell whether it really improved.
    • The AI is not consistent on hard cases. On 105 borderline points, running the same AI twice changed the answer on 15 (about 1 in 7); two different AIs disagreed on 24.
    • An AI checking an AI is not enough. The standard advice is to compare AI graders with people at least once. Some points you see were kept by the AI and some were thrown out; you are not told which. If your answers match the AI's on at least 9 in 10, the key is used as it is; otherwise we fix how it is made first.

    What you do

    1. 1You see 20 points, one at a time. Each says who the law affects and how.
    2. 2Read the highlighted sentence from the official report.
    3. 3Decide: does that sentence back up the point?
    Yes, keepThe report says it. No, dropThe report doesn't really say it. For example: it is only background, it is about an option the EU did not pick, it is too vague, a number is wrong, or the point repeats another one. Can't tellYou can't decide. Write a few words why.
    See two short examples

    Point: Online shops must label AI-made product photos and pay for tools to do it.

    Report: "... online retailers will incur one-off costs to adapt their systems to the labelling obligation ..."

    Yes, keep: the report says these shops pay to comply.

    Point: Banks must hire more compliance staff.

    Report: "... Option 3, which was not retained, would have required banks to appoint additional officers ..."

    No, drop: that option was not chosen, so it is not an effect of the law.

    Help us check how our AI is graded

    Our AI writes a report on who a new EU law affects and how. A grader AI scores it against the effects the EU itself lists. An AI cannot be trusted to check another AI, so a person must check the grader before we use its scores. That is you.

    Why a person has to look

    • Every score comes from the grader. If it is too generous or too strict, we measure our AI's progress wrongly, and may keep a worse version or throw away a better one.
    • Graders make predictable mistakes: counting an effect as found because the words look alike, or as missed because the words differ.
    • So we compare it with people once. You and another person label the same pairs on your own, without seeing what the grader decided. If the grader agrees with you at least 85% of the time, we use it; otherwise we fix it first.

    What you do

    1. 1You see 30 pairs, one at a time: one effect from the EU's report, and our AI's report on the same law. A computer is easiest (Ctrl+F helps).
    2. 2Look through our AI's report for that effect.
    3. 3Decide: does our AI's report mention it?
    Yes, coveredSome entry names the same group and the same effect, in any words. No, not coveredNo entry does. Go by what it wrote, not what it might have meant. Can't tellYou can't decide. Write a few words why.

    Ignore missing amounts (euros, percentages, staff numbers): our AI never saw those figures. Work alone, and don't discuss pairs with the other person until you have both sent.

    See two short examples

    Effect: Online shops must label AI-made product photos and pay EUR 2,000 each for new tools.

    Our AI wrote: "Retailers selling online face compliance costs to mark synthetic images."

    Yes, covered: same group, same way; the missing amount doesn't matter.

    Effect: National authorities must supervise the new product register and need extra staff.

    Our AI wrote: "Companies must register their products before sale."

    No, not covered: that entry is about companies registering, not authorities supervising.
