Help us check an AI-written answer key

Our AI lists who a new EU law affects and how. An AI judge decides what counts as a right answer, based on the EU's official reports. We need your review to calibrate it.

Why a person has to look

  • Every grade depends on the key. A wrong key rewards our AI for copying its mistakes, and we could not tell whether it really improved.
  • The AI is not consistent on hard cases. On 105 borderline points, running the same AI twice changed the answer on 15 (about 1 in 7); two different AIs disagreed on 24.
  • An AI checking an AI is not enough. The standard advice is to compare AI graders with people at least once. The AI has answered every point you see; you are not told how. Your answers show whether the AI reads its sources the way people do; the answer key itself is reviewed separately.

What you do

  1. 1You see your points, one at a time. Each says who the law affects and how.
  2. 2Read the highlighted sentence from the official report.
  3. 3Decide: is the point an accurate reading of that sentence?
YesEvery group, effect and amount in the claim is supported by the source. NoThe claim adds or changes something the source does not support, for example a number the report doesn't give, or an option the EU did not pick. Can't tellYou need more information to decide. Briefly explain what is missing.
See three short examples

Point: Online shops face one-off costs to adapt their systems to the labelling duty.

Report: "... online retailers will incur one-off costs to adapt their systems to the labelling obligation ..."

Yes: same group, same effect, nothing added.

Point: Banks must hire more compliance staff.

Report: "... Option 3, which was not retained, would have required banks to appoint additional officers ..."

No: that option was not chosen, so it is not an effect of the law.

Point: Online shops face one-off costs of about EUR 5,000 to adapt their systems.

Report: "... online retailers will incur one-off costs to adapt their systems to the labelling obligation ..."

No: the report gives no amount, so the EUR 5,000 is added.

Help us check how our AI is graded

Our AI writes a report on who a new EU law affects and how. An AI judge scores it against the effects the EU itself lists. We need your review to calibrate it.

Why a person has to look

  • Every score comes from the grader. If it is too generous or too strict, we measure our AI's progress wrongly, and may keep a worse version or throw away a better one.
  • Graders make predictable mistakes: counting an effect as found because the words look alike, or as missed because the words differ.
  • So we compare it with people once. You and another person label the same pairs on your own, without seeing what the grader decided. If the grader agrees with you at least 85% of the time, we use it; otherwise we fix it first.

What you do

  1. 1You see your pairs, one at a time: one effect from the EU's report, and our AI's report on the same law. A computer is easiest (Ctrl+F helps).
  2. 2Look through our AI's report for that effect.
  3. 3Decide: does our AI's report mention it?
Yes, coveredSome entry names the same group and the same effect, in any words. No, not coveredNo entry does. Go by what it wrote, not what it might have meant. Can't tellYou can't decide. Write a few words why.

Ignore missing amounts (euros, percentages, staff numbers): our AI never saw those figures. Work alone, and don't discuss pairs with the other person until you have both sent.
